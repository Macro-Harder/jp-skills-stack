# The Charge

This skill distils rule R-54, The charge, in LEVEL-3-TEAMS.md of the build flow documents. Where this skill and the source disagree, the source wins.

## Purpose

A round that starts without agreed terms drifts: scope grows, criteria shift, and verdicts cannot be traced to what was asked. The charge fixes the terms in writing before examination begins, so every verdict can cite the basis it was issued under.

## Procedure

1. Draft the charge before any examination begins.
2. Agree it with three parties: the producing team, the Project Manager, and the Auditor, who chairs the round.
3. Name in the charge: the scope of the round, its success criteria, its agenda, the audit criteria it will judge against, and the board published list of materials it will inspect.
4. Open the round only on the agreed charge.
5. Require every verdict the round issues to cite the charge it was issued under.
6. Amend with care during the round. The chair may amend the charge only as to scope and roster, and each amendment and its reason is recorded.
7. Protect the fixed parts. The audit criteria and the success criteria may not be amended after examination has begun. Changing either one requires a new charge and a new round.
8. Apply no amendment retroactively. Examination done under a superseded scope is reported as done under that scope.

## Edge cases

- Agreeing the charge fixes scope and criteria only. It does not pre-clear, approve, or veto who may be staffed, and it is not a condition a spin-up must satisfy before working.
- If a needed change touches the audit criteria or the success criteria mid round, stop that line, raise a new charge, and start a new round for it.
- If a verdict arrives with no charge cited, return it. The verdict is not complete until its charge is named.
- If scope was amended, reports must keep the old and new scope results separate, each labelled with the scope it was examined under.
