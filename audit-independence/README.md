# Audit Independence

## What it is

Audit Independence is the practice of proving an auditor can judge fairly before the audit begins. It rests on a simple shift: instead of asking auditors to promise they are unbiased, it asks them to name the specific pressures that could bias them and to deal with each one in writing.

Five threats are named for every engagement. Self-interest covers financial or career gain tied to the outcome. Self-review covers judging work the auditor helped create. Advocacy covers promoting a position the auditor has already defended. Familiarity covers close relationships that soften scrutiny. Intimidation covers pressure that makes honest findings hard to state.

Each threat gets one disposition only. It is eliminated, it is reduced to an acceptable level by a named safeguard, or the engagement is declined. Self-review sits apart from the others. It is prohibited, not manageable, because a person cannot credibly check their own work by adding a safeguard around it.

The naming happens at the charge step that opens the round, led by the Auditor as chair, and it is written as a threat naming record inside the round record. A verifier outside the assessed team checks every disposition other than self-review. What counts as acceptable is that verifier judgement. Without that check, a disposition does not stand, and no audit work before the record exists counts toward the round.

## Example

A team is asked to audit a release it helped design. At the charge step the Auditor names self-review and records it as present. Because the team built part of what it would judge, the threat cannot be eliminated by swapping one member. The engagement is declined for that team and reassigned. In the same round, familiarity is named for a second auditor who knows the builders well. The safeguard is rotation to a different workstream plus outside review of findings. The outside verifier records that this reduces the threat to an acceptable level, and the audit proceeds.

## How to use it

Use the folder at the start of an audit round. The Auditor supplies the engagement context: who is auditing, what is being audited, and any prior involvement. The output is a single threat naming record with five rows, one per threat, each with a disposition and, where needed, a named safeguard and an outside check. File it in the round record before any examination.

## For agents

An agent acting as Auditor must produce the record itself, not a summary of intent. An agent acting as verifier must sit outside the assessed team and record a judgement per disposition. Agents must not accept good faith, effort, or past performance as a safeguard, and must halt counting work if the record is missing or unchecked.
