# Count and Total Reconciliation

## Define the counting unit first

- Write down what one counted item is before counting, in plain words, such as one distinct agent, one spin up event, one tickable checklist unit or one audit place.

- Write down what is excluded, such as scheduled jobs when agents are being counted, already counted standing roles when step teams are being counted, and stale or finished entries.

- Check whether the user asked for distinct items or for every occurrence, because the two give different totals for the same work and swapping them caused a reported total to be corrected in this window.

- Keep one counting unit for the whole total. Stop and restart the count when the unit changes part way through.

## Derive the total two ways

- Build the total from its parts and show the addition, naming each part and its subtotal so a reader can recount without redoing the search.

- Recount by a second route that does not reuse the first route, such as counting the underlying list directly after deriving the total from a formula or a table.

- Watch for the double count shapes that recurred here, such as a per loop figure reported as a whole flow figure, a shared item counted in two scopes, and a total copied from an earlier tier or version after the inputs changed.

- Compare the result against every other place the same total already appears, and fix every copy when the recount changes it instead of fixing only the copy in front of you.

## Report the total with its derivation

- Report the total together with its counting unit, its parts and the source the parts were counted from, never as a bare number.

- Label a figure as measured, derived or estimated, and say which parts rest on a guess when any part does.

- Refuse to carry a total you could not recount, and report it as not verified with the missing source named, rather than repeating a number from an earlier report, a board or a summary.

- Record the derivation beside the total in the project record, so the next update recounts from the same unit instead of inventing a new one.

## Alternatives for better results

- Shrink the claim when the full recount is too large, and report the smaller part you did recount with its unit named, leaving the rest marked unverified.

- Ask the user which counting unit they mean when distinct items and every occurrence are both plausible and the choice changes the decision the total feeds.
