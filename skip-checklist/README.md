# Skip Checklist

## What it is
This skill is used before adopting or skipping a move inside a build flow step, when a move cannot be performed properly, and when a step record claims or omits a skip. A move is a unit inside a step. Flow steps, Auditor gates, and stage closures are out of scope and are never skipped under this skill. The skill runs six questions, applies verdict rules and a never skip override, and records any skip. It is never for flow steps, Auditor gates, stage closure, or pattern decisions as such.

The procedure starts with entry routing, stating whether the run is adopting or skipping, plus the move and its hosting step. It then asks on the record whether the hosting decision is irreversible. Three hard blocks can end the path. A flow step, Auditor gate, or stage closure is never the subject. The Framer, the Breaker, and the Trade off Judge are never skipped on an irreversible decision, and if any is skipped the decision is not taken, is recorded as not taken, and the move runs before the decision is taken again. A move whose output is the hosting step closure evidence, or that a numbered rule requires at that step, is not skippable.

If the team could not perform the move properly, that is recorded as a gap, not as a skip, and the skip verdict rules do not run. For unblocked, non gap runs, the six questions are answered in order: whether the problem is stated in one sentence with a metric or done condition, whether a cheaper move was tried first, whether the owner of the move and the owner of what it produces are named, whether new failure modes are listed with at least one actually tested, whether the consequence is written down covering cost, consistency, reversibility, who pays, and when, and whether an exit or revisit trigger is named.

The verdict rules follow from the answers. On an adopting run, any "no" means do not adopt it yet. On a skipping run for an irreversible decision, any "no" means do not skip it. If the checklist passes and the move still would not change the decision, the move is skipped and the trigger that would bring it back is recorded. Where no stated rule produces a verdict, the record reads "No stated verdict" with the stated flag. A skip is recorded in the step record with its reason naming its ground and two separately labelled trigger lines, "exit or revisit trigger" and "the trigger that would bring it back". A skip is never entered in the decision queue. A claimed skip is checked against the run record elements, and a claim missing elements or lacking a run record is recorded as an unverified claim. At step record drafting and handover, an absence strip compares charged moves against claimed moves, so each charged move shows as done, a recorded skip, a recorded gap, or an unrecorded absence, with a count check over the categories.

## Example
A step team proposes skipping a move on a decision the step record marks irreversible, and the move is one of the three protected moves, the Framer, the Breaker, or the Trade off Judge. The run states the entry as skipping, records the irreversibility answer, and the never skip block applies. The skip does not proceed, the decision is recorded as not taken, and the move must run before the decision is taken again. The block is recorded in the step record, naming the gate and the move.

## How to use it
Place the folder in an agent skills library. The agent loads the skill at the points the skill lists, including when a move in a step is about to be adopted or skipped, when a move cannot be performed properly, when a step record is about to claim a skipped move, and at step record drafting and handover when a charged move is absent without a recorded skip. A person can also ask directly in plain words, for example by asking for the skip checklist to be run on a named move in a named step.

The inputs are sought, not guaranteed, and no input gates a run. They are the move and its hosting step, the entry stated before verdict, the irreversibility determination, the facts the six questions ask about, the hosting step charge and closure requirements, the charged moves list and its source for the absence strip, whether the team could perform the move properly, and the step record or working record. Where a demanded thing does not exist, the honest answer is "no".

## For agents
When the skill loads, the agent follows the steps in order: entry routing, the irreversibility question, the three hard blocks, gap screening, the six questions, the verdict rules, recording and the claim check, and the absence strip. The agent never resolves the source open points, never decides pattern adoption, refusal, or parking as such, and routes pattern decisions to the pattern apparatus the skill names. Where move and pattern cannot be told apart, the agent runs this checklist and records the classification as uncertain.
