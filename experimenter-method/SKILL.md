# experimenter-method

## Purpose

Turn each contested, load-bearing assumption behind an idea into a falsifiable test: state the hypothesis, the metric, and the result that would prove the idea wrong, all fixed before the run, with selector criteria fixed in advance; then judge the run only against the terms fixed beforehand. At the judging entry point the same gate applies, as a check inside step 5 from the plan record in input 4: a run testing an assumption not listed in the plan as contested and load-bearing must not receive a pass. Name any run arranged to succeed a demo, not an experiment.

This skill distils LEVEL-2-STEPS.md, Step 5 methods. Where this skill and that source disagree, the source wins.

Definitions:

- contested: disputed, uncertain, or not agreed, rather than a settled fact.
- load-bearing: the idea depends on it, so that if it is wrong the idea fails or changes materially.
- assumption: a belief taken as true without proof in the current work, which the idea relies on.
- falsifiable test: a test set up so that a possible outcome can show the assumption or idea is wrong, not only outcomes that confirm it.
- hypothesis: the specific expected outcome stated before the test, in a form that can be checked against what actually happens.
- metric: the specific measurement used to judge the outcome, including what is counted and how it is counted.
- falsifying result: the specific outcome that, if observed, means the idea or assumption has failed.
- selector criteria: the explicit rule for choosing which cases or examples are included in the test, and which are excluded.
- demo: a display or run arranged to show the idea working, typically on favourable cases.
- designed to succeed: set up, selected, or tuned so that a successful outcome is expected or arranged, rather than letting the outcome decide the result.
- experiment: a test in which the idea could genuinely fail, because the hypothesis, metric, falsifying result, and selector criteria were fixed in advance and the outcome was not arranged.

### Procedure

The five steps run in order, with no branches inside a run. Choosing the entry point before the run starts, by whether the run has already happened, is an entry-point dispatch, not a branch inside the procedure: it selects the starting step, and once started, the steps run in order with no branching. The judging job starts at step 5 and does not run steps 1 to 4. The stop rules are exits that end the work at the point a missing input is discovered, before the run; they are not branches inside these five steps. Stop rule 2 applies only at the design entry point, so a completed run offered as proof reaches step 5 without stop rule 2 firing.

#### Step 1: List each contested, load-bearing assumption behind the idea

Leave out assumptions that are not contested or not load-bearing. Do not test only the assumptions that are easy or safe. Do not accept a presenter's word that an assumption is settled. The source defines contested as disputed, uncertain, or not agreed, rather than a settled fact, so contested is a property shown by disagreement or uncertainty, not a label the presenter controls. Treat an assumption as contested only where one of two grounds holds, and the same bar applies to both grounds. The stated reason for a disagreement and the stated dependency for an omission are the same kind of grounding, a reason the assumption is in doubt or the idea depends on it.

First ground, stated disagreement: disagreement or uncertainty counts only where the material in input 2 records it as a statement by a named party, or as a recorded dispute or uncertainty in the material itself. The threshold is one named party who states disagreement or uncertainty with a stated reason, or one recorded dispute or uncertainty in the material with a stated reason; a bare assertion with no reason does not meet the threshold. A stated reason counts as sufficient only where it follows from the claim or the context in input 3 and names the specific ground: it must state a ground beyond restating the disagreement or uncertainty, such as the evidence, observation, or link that gives the party cause to doubt the assumption. A reason that merely repeats the disagreement or uncertainty, or states no ground beyond naming it, does not meet the threshold, and a reason that does not follow from the claim or the context in input 3 does not meet the threshold either. A vacuous or circular reason fails the threshold the same way a vacuous dependency fails. The agent's own doubt alone does not count.

Second ground, omission: an unstated load-bearing assumption counts as contested only where the agent names it from the claim and the context in input 3 and states the dependency, that is, why the idea depends on it; a bare naming with no stated dependency does not meet the threshold, just as a bare assertion with no reason does not meet the threshold for stated disagreement. A stated dependency counts as sufficient only where it follows from the claim and the context in input 3 and names the specific link: if the assumption were wrong, the idea fails or changes materially, stated in terms of the claim and the context. A dependency that merely repeats the assumption, or states no link beyond naming it, does not meet the threshold, and a dependency that does not follow from the claim and the context in input 3 does not meet the threshold either.

The material in input 2 and the list form of input 2 are taken as given: the skill makes no authenticity check on them, so a presenter could manufacture contested status by writing a disagreement into the material or the list, or exempt an assumption by supplying material with no disagreement in it. That is a recorded limitation, not a check the agent performs: the challenge rule tests what the material records, not whether the material is genuine. Where input 2 is a list of assumptions rather than material, the list itself counts as the material: the threshold is applied to the list as written, so a listed assumption counts as contested only where the list records a named party stating disagreement or uncertainty with a stated reason, or a recorded dispute or uncertainty with a stated reason, and a bare list with no such record means the threshold is not met for the listed assumptions.

Then run a completeness check: ask what the idea depends on that the material never states, and list any such unstated load-bearing assumption as contested only where the omission ground is met. Limitation, recorded here because the source gives no completeness rule: the skill can only surface omissions the agent can name from the claim and the context in input 3; it cannot prove the material is complete, and a load-bearing assumption no one names still escapes testing.

#### Step 2: Fix hypothesis, metric, and falsifying result before the run

For each listed assumption, fix before the run the hypothesis (the specific expected outcome if the idea is right), the metric (the specific measurement used to judge the outcome, including what is counted and how it is counted), and the falsifying result (the specific outcome that, if observed, means the idea or assumption has failed).

The hypothesis counts as specific enough only if it names an outcome checkable against what actually happens; a vague expectation such as things getting better does not count, and if no checkable outcome can be stated the lock cannot be fixed, so stop rule 2 applies. Check that the hypothesis outcome is also a possible, observable outcome, by the same test as the falsifying result below.

The metric counts as specific enough only if it names what is counted and how it is counted in a way checkable against what actually happens; a vague metric such as engagement with no counting rule does not count, and if no checkable metric can be stated the lock cannot be fixed, so stop rule 2 applies.

The falsifying result counts as specific enough only if it names an outcome checkable against what actually happens; a vague falsifying result such as things get worse does not count, and if no checkable outcome can be stated the lock cannot be fixed, so stop rule 2 applies. Check that the falsifying result is a possible, observable outcome: it must be an outcome that could actually occur and could be observed if it did. A falsifying result that cannot occur or cannot be observed does not satisfy step 2, and the lock cannot be fixed, so stop rule 2 applies.

Check that the metric can observe both outcomes and tell them apart: the metric must count or measure the thing that would show the hypothesis outcome if it occurred, and the thing that would show the falsifying result if it occurred. The check works by naming, for each outcome, what the metric would record if that outcome occurred; the two records must differ in a way that identifies which outcome occurred. If the metric would record the same value for both outcomes, or record nothing for an outcome, or cannot detect an outcome at all, the lock cannot be fixed, so stop rule 2 applies.

Check that the hypothesis outcome and the falsifying result are mutually exclusive: no single observed result can match both. If the two outcomes overlap, the lock cannot be fixed, so stop rule 2 applies.

Record all three in writing before the run.

#### Step 3: Fix selector criteria in advance

Fix the selector criteria in advance: the explicit rule for choosing which cases, examples, inputs, or subjects the test will use, and which are excluded. The rule counts as explicit enough only if it can be applied without judgement about which cases favour the idea: it must name the inclusion and exclusion conditions in a way that picks the cases before the results are known, with no discretion left to favour the idea. A vague rule such as representative cases does not count, and if no such rule can be stated the lock cannot be fixed, so stop rule 2 applies. Record the rule in writing before the run.

#### Step 4: Record the written test plan before the run

Record the written test plan before the run, with the date and time it was recorded: the listed assumptions, and for each a falsifiable test with hypothesis, metric, and falsifying result, plus the selector criteria. The plan carries per-lock timing evidence: each lock carries the date and time it first appears in the plan, either as a timestamp on the lock itself or as a dated version history showing when each lock first appears. A plan recorded as a single version with one overall recording date and time, and with no revision history, evidences every lock contained in that plan as first appearing at that recording date and time, and such a single-version plan counts as a dated version history with one version for the timing check. The recording date and time are the evidence step 5 uses for when each lock in the plan was fixed; where the plan is revised before the run, the latest recording time before the run is the evidence for the locks present in that version, and a lock is evidenced only by the version in which it first appears. The version history must show the term content of each version, not only when each lock first appears, so the terms in the latest version recorded before the run can be established. The fixed terms for each lock are those in the version in which the lock first appears, or, where a later version recorded before the run changed them, those in the latest version recorded before the run. A lock first appearing in a revision recorded after the run counts as fixed after the run. A change to a lock's terms in a revision recorded after the run is disregarded for judging, because a post-run revision is not a valid plan version: the verdict uses the terms in the latest version recorded before the run, so a hypothesis revised after the run to match the observed result cannot produce a pass. Nothing in the plan is written or changed after the run as a design act: a post-run revision is not a valid plan version, it is evidence that the lock it introduces was fixed after the run.

#### Step 5: Judge the result only against the terms fixed beforehand

After the run, judge the result only against the terms fixed beforehand, in the latest version of the plan recorded before the run. At the judging entry point, start here, using input 4 only. The run is judged against the fixed terms only where every lock was fixed before the run and no demo condition applies, and it is judged demo where any lock was not fixed before the run or any other demo condition applies, as the precedence order below states; there is no partial judging against the subset of locks that were fixed.

Check the timing of each lock, lock by lock, against the dated written record of the test plan in step 4, compared with the run time supplied in input 4, using the per-lock evidence in step 4: the date and time each lock first appears, from its timestamp or from the dated version history. A single overall recording timestamp on a plan with no revision history counts as the date and time each lock in that plan first appears. The dated record overrides stated timing where they conflict: if the offerer states a lock was fixed before the run and the record shows it after the run, or shows no such lock, the record wins and the lock counts as fixed after the run or never fixed, as the record shows. The plan record is taken as given, and the skill makes no authenticity check on it: the skill trusts the record for the fixed terms, the gate listing, and the timing, and the dated record overrides stated timing, so a fabricated backdated record could pass every judging check. That is a recorded limitation, not a check the agent performs. A lock first appearing in a revision recorded after the run counts as fixed after the run, so demo applies. A change to a lock's terms in a revision recorded after the run is disregarded for judging, and the verdict uses the terms in the latest version recorded before the run; the change is not itself a demo condition, because the lock was fixed before the run. Where no dated written record of the plan exists, or no run time is recorded, the timing of a lock cannot be verified, and that lock is treated as not shown fixed before the run.

Check arrangement next, from the arrangement facts in input 4 only. A run counts as arranged to succeed where any one of these is shown in those facts: only favourable cases were used; cases were chosen after seeing the results; failure was made impossible by the setup or by tuning; or the setup was selected or tuned so that a successful outcome was expected or arranged, rather than letting the outcome decide the result. Do not accept the presenter's word that the run was not arranged: a label counts for nothing against facts showing one of the tests above. Where arrangement cannot be determined from the facts supplied, because the facts are absent, thin, or disputed with no resolving evidence, judge the run "demo, not an experiment": an experiment requires a genuine chance to fail, and where that cannot be shown the run cannot count as an experiment.

Check the contested and load-bearing gate next, from the plan record supplied in input 4: the assumption behind the run, named in input 4, must be one listed in that plan as contested and load-bearing. If it is not listed as contested and load-bearing in the plan, the gate fails, so the run must not receive a pass. Where the plan lists more than one assumption, the named assumption is the one the gate check tests. It is equivalently the assumption whose test terms the observed result is judged against: the hypothesis outcome and falsifying result the result is compared with are that assumption's terms.

Check the quality bars next, against the fixed terms as recorded in the latest version of the plan recorded before the run in input 4, applying the step 2 and step 3 bars: the hypothesis must name an outcome checkable against what actually happens and be a possible, observable outcome; the metric must name what is counted and how it is counted in a way checkable against what actually happens, and must observe both outcomes and tell them apart by recording values that differ in a way that identifies which outcome occurred; the falsifying result must name an outcome checkable against what actually happens and be a possible, observable outcome; the hypothesis outcome and the falsifying result must be mutually exclusive; and the selector criteria must be applicable without judgement about which cases favour the idea. A lock fixed before the run that fails any of those bars was fixed in time but not fixed to the quality bars, so it cannot be judged pass or fail determinately.

Verdict precedence, in order. First, demo: if the run was arranged to succeed under the test above, or arrangement cannot be determined from the facts supplied, judge it "demo, not an experiment", never a pass and never a fail; if any single lock was fixed after the run, including selector criteria fixed after seeing the results and including a lock first appearing in a post-run revision, judge it "demo, not an experiment"; if any lock was never fixed at all, judge it "demo, not an experiment"; if the timing of a lock cannot be verified, treat that lock as not shown fixed before the run and judge it "demo, not an experiment". The demo judgement overrides pass, fail, and no verdict. Second, no verdict: where no demo condition applies, give no verdict where the assumption behind the run was not listed in the plan as contested and load-bearing, recording that the gate failed; or where a lock fixed before the run fails a step 2 or step 3 quality bar, recording which bar failed, because the defect is in the fixed terms and was discovered only after the run, while demo is reserved for arrangement or timing failures; or where the observed result matches neither the hypothesis outcome nor the falsifying result, or where it matches both, or where the run yields no observable result at all. The no-verdict rules apply only where no demo condition applies. That one condition covers all five grounds alike: the gate, the quality bars, and the neither, both, and no-result outcomes. Undetermined arrangement is a demo condition, so it bars no verdict just as a late lock does. Where more than one no-verdict ground applies, the record names the first ground in this order: gate failure first, then quality-bar failure, then outcome match, whether neither, both, or no result. The verdict is no verdict in every combination; only the record follows the order. Where the quality ground is the mutual-exclusivity bar, shown by a both match, the record states both that both fixed outcomes matched and that the terms were not mutually exclusive, so the one record covers both grounds. Third, pass or fail: where no demo condition applies, every lock passes the quality bars, and the gate passes, pass where the hypothesis outcome was observed, or fail where the falsifying result was observed.

### Edge cases

Verdict precedence, stated in full in step 5, governs every overlap below: demo first, then no verdict, then pass or fail, and where more than one no-verdict ground applies, the record names the first ground in step 5's order.

#### Assumptions left out of the plan

- Contested but not load-bearing: the assumption is left out of the test plan. The skill tests only assumptions that are both contested and load-bearing. A disputed detail with no link to whether the idea works, such as the colour of headings in a checklist, is not tested.
- Load-bearing but not contested: the assumption is left out of the test plan, but only after the step 1 challenge check. A settled fact is not turned into a test. An assumption is treated as settled only where no contested evidence meets the step 1 threshold: the material records no named party stating disagreement or uncertainty with a stated reason, and no recorded dispute or uncertainty with a stated reason, and no omission ground meets the equivalent threshold. There are only two states for the skill: contested, where the threshold is met and the assumption is tested, and not contested, where the threshold is not met and the assumption is left out. An assumption neither disputed nor agreed, stated neutrally, is not contested, so it is left out and treated as settled for the purposes of this skill.
- Assumption omitted from the material entirely: it is listed in step 1 if the completeness check names it as load-bearing and states the dependency, that is, why the idea depends on it, naming the specific link in terms of the claim and the context. A bare naming with no stated dependency does not meet the threshold, so the assumption is left out. An omission no one names still escapes testing.
- Assumption presented as settled fact when it is contested: do not accept the presenter's word that an assumption is settled. If the material meets the step 1 threshold, for example a named party states disagreement with a stated reason that follows from the claim and the context, the assumption is listed as contested and load-bearing and a falsifiable test is designed for it. Contested is a property shown by disagreement or uncertainty, not a label the presenter controls.
- Missing or vague context: if the context given does not let the agent tell whether an assumption is load-bearing, stop rule 1 applies. No assumption can then be named as load-bearing, so the skill says so and stops. It does not go looking for something to test.

#### A lock that cannot be fixed

- If the hypothesis, metric, falsifying result, or selector criteria cannot be fixed before the run, stop rule 2 applies at the design entry point. The skill says so and stops. The run must not proceed as an experiment. At best it is a demo, and a demo is not evidence the idea holds.
- A vague hypothesis names no outcome checkable against what actually happens, for example "things get better". A vague falsifying result names no checkable outcome, for example "things get worse". A vague metric names neither what is counted nor how it is counted, for example "engagement" with no counting rule. Vague selector criteria, for example "representative cases", cannot be applied without judgement about which cases favour the idea. In each case, if no checkable version can be stated, the lock cannot be fixed.
- A falsifying result or a hypothesis outcome that cannot occur, for example ticket counts falling below zero, or that cannot be observed if it occurred, for example users silently losing trust, does not satisfy the lock. The lock cannot be fixed.
- A metric that does not observe the falsifying result cannot fix the lock. If the metric records ticket counts but records nothing about checklist abandonment, it cannot detect a falsifying result stated as abandonment. If the metric records the same value for both outcomes, for example only the count of accounts enrolled, it cannot tell the two outcomes apart. If the hypothesis outcome and the falsifying result overlap, so one observed result could match both, the lock cannot be fixed.

#### A run arranged to succeed

- Judge a run arranged to succeed as "demo, not an experiment". It is never recorded as a pass, and never recorded as a fail, even where the falsifying result was observed. A demonstration set up to show the idea working does not count as a test, even if it looks like one.
- Arrangement is judged from the arrangement facts in input 4 only, by the operational test: only favourable cases were used; cases were chosen after seeing the results; failure was made impossible by the setup or by tuning; or the setup was selected or tuned so that a successful outcome was expected or arranged, rather than letting the outcome decide the result. A presenter's word that the run was not arranged counts for nothing against facts showing one of these.
- Where arrangement cannot be determined from the facts supplied, because the facts are absent, thin, or disputed with no resolving evidence, judge the run "demo, not an experiment" as well. An experiment requires a genuine chance to fail, and where that cannot be shown the run cannot count as an experiment.

#### Timing of the locks

- A single late lock, a lock never fixed, and a lock first appearing in a revision recorded after the run each draw the demo judgement under step 5; there is no partial judging against the subset of locks that were fixed. Timing is checked lock by lock, using the date and time each lock first appears in the dated written plan record or its dated version history, compared with the run time.
- Where no prior plan was supplied, the output is the demo verdict plus a statement that no plan was supplied.
- Stated timing conflicts with the dated record: the dated record wins, as step 5 states.
- Timing unverifiable, where no dated written plan record exists or no run time is recorded: the lock is treated as not shown fixed before the run, and where no record exists the fixed terms are not supplied either, so the demo judgement applies on the unverifiable timing ground.

#### A pre-run lock revised post-run

- A lock fixed before the run whose terms were changed in a revision recorded after the run is judged against the terms in the latest version of the plan recorded before the run, and the post-run change is disregarded. The lock was fixed before the run, so the change is not itself a demo condition. A hypothesis revised after the run to match the observed result cannot produce a pass: if the observed result matches the revised hypothesis but not the pre-run hypothesis outcome, the result is judged against the pre-run terms, so it is a neither outcome where it matches neither pre-run outcome, or a fail where it matches the pre-run falsifying result.
- Where only a post-run revised record is supplied, with no pre-run version or version history showing the pre-run terms, the pre-run terms are not supplied, so no pass or fail can be judged, and the demo judgement applies on the unverifiable ground, as where no dated written plan record exists.
- Where a pre-run revision changed a lock, the verdict uses the latest version recorded before the run, not the version in which the lock first appears. A version history must show the term content of each version, not only when each lock first appears, so the latest pre-run terms can be established.

#### The gate at judging

- A run testing an assumption not listed as contested and load-bearing in the plan record: at the judging entry point, check the gate from the plan record supplied in input 4. If the assumption behind the run, named in input 4, was not listed in that plan as contested and load-bearing, give no verdict, and record that the gate failed, so the run must not receive a pass. This is not step 1 and it is not stop rule 1 firing: step 1 never runs at judging, and the outcome is no verdict, not a stop. Where a demo condition also applies, the demo judgement overrides.
- Input 4 naming no assumption: where input 4 names no assumption, the gate check cannot show the assumption listed as contested and load-bearing, so give no verdict and record that the gate failed. The run must not receive a pass.

#### Quality bars at judging

- A lock fixed before the run that fails a step 2 or step 3 quality bar cannot be judged pass or fail determinately, so give no verdict, and record which bar failed. This is no verdict rather than demo: the run was a genuine attempt with terms fixed before the run, and the defect in the fixed terms was discovered only after the run, while demo is reserved for arrangement or timing failures. Where a demo condition also applies, the demo judgement overrides.
- The bars are: the hypothesis must name an outcome checkable against what actually happens and be a possible, observable outcome; the metric must name what is counted and how it is counted in a way checkable against what actually happens, and must observe both outcomes and tell them apart by recording values that differ in a way that identifies which outcome occurred; the falsifying result must name an outcome checkable against what actually happens and be a possible, observable outcome; the hypothesis outcome and the falsifying result must be mutually exclusive; and the selector criteria must be applicable without judgement about which cases favour the idea.
- A vague hypothesis, a vague or unobservable falsifying result, a vague metric, a metric that does not observe the falsifying result, a metric that records the same value for both outcomes, an impossible hypothesis or falsifying result, and vague selector criteria are each a quality-bar failure at judging, giving no verdict with the failed bar recorded. A fresh test, with new terms fixed before its run, is the only way to test the idea again.

#### The observed result

- A result matching neither the hypothesis outcome nor the falsifying result: give no verdict, where no demo condition applies, and record that the outcome was outside the fixed terms. The source gives verdicts for two outcomes only, so no third verdict is added, and a neither outcome is not a fail, because it is not the falsifying result.
- A result matching both fixed outcomes: give no verdict, where no demo condition applies, and record both statements step 5 names. A both match shows the plan failed step 2's mutual-exclusivity bar, a defect in the fixed terms discovered only after the run, so the outcome is no verdict, not demo.
- A run with no observable result at all: give no verdict, where no demo condition applies, and record that no result was observed. No result is not a neither outcome: the measurement failed or no data was recorded, so nothing can be judged pass or fail.
- In each case, do not re-judge the result against new terms after the fact. A fresh test, with new terms fixed before its run, is the only way to test the idea again.

#### Overlapping verdict conditions

- Every overlap in this section is disposed by step 5's precedence: a demo condition, including a late lock and undetermined arrangement, overrides no verdict and fail alike; co-occurring no-verdict grounds leave the verdict at no verdict, with the record naming the first ground in step 5's order; and a both match is recorded with both statements step 5 names, the match and the failed mutual-exclusivity bar.
- A gate failure is not cured by a fresh test of the same assumption unless that assumption is shown contested and load-bearing.

### Never do

- Never invent variants of the procedure by test type or domain. The source gives no variants.
- Never add a statistical method for analysing results. The skill judges pass or fail against the pre-fixed falsifying result.
- Never write instructions for running the test. The skill designs and judges the test; the run is the work that follows the skill.
- Never choose what to test when no contested, load-bearing assumption can be named. Stop rule 1 applies.
- Never fix, change, or swap a hypothesis, metric, falsifying result, or selector criteria after the run, or after seeing the result. A post-run change is disregarded for judging, and the verdict uses the terms in the latest version recorded before the run.
- Never judge a result against anything other than the terms fixed beforehand.
- Never re-describe a bad result as a success.
- Never drop cases that fail, or choose cases after seeing the results.
- Never call a demo an experiment, and never treat a demo as evidence the idea holds.
- Never add branches to the procedure inside a run, and never add a top-level section to the SKILL.md beyond the house format. Choosing the entry point before the run starts, by whether the run has already happened, is allowed expressly: it is an entry-point dispatch that selects the starting step, not a branch inside the procedure, and once started the steps run in order with no branching.

### Boundary

This skill loads when the work is to design or judge a falsifiable test of a contested, load-bearing assumption, with the test terms fixed before the run. That covers an idea resting on disputed, load-bearing assumptions, a decision turning on such an assumption, deciding before a run what would count as the idea being wrong, a demo offered as proof, and a completed run offered as proof. A neighbour loads instead when the work is only the neighbour's job, and this skill never loads for a neighbour's job alone.

- live-page-checks loads instead when the whole job is checking a current price, stock level, or availability from a page. This skill never loads when the job is only a standalone page check.
- wide-research loads instead when the job is gathering the same fields across many independent inputs, with no falsifying result at stake. This skill never loads when the job is only a bulk gather.
- documents loads instead when the job is only writing up or revising a file. This skill never loads when the job is only a file write-up.
- handoff-timing loads instead when the job is only formatting a timing line for a counting report. This skill never loads when the job is only a timing line.

Where the work is to design or judge a falsifiable test, none of the four neighbours loads, because none of them designs a test, fixes terms before a run, or judges a demo against an experiment. The test plan and verdict produced by this skill are written in the fixed form set out in this skill; that written form is part of this skill's output, not a handoff to a neighbour.

Evidence gathering for a test is unassigned: where gathering serves a test, with a falsifying result at stake, neither this skill nor wide-research loads for the gathering itself, under the E3 deferral to the next version. This skill does not gather evidence, and wide-research loads only when no falsifying result is at stake.

## Input

1. The claim or idea being tested.
2. The contested, load-bearing assumptions behind it, or the material in which they can be found.
3. The context in which the idea will be used, enough to tell whether an assumption is load-bearing.
4. The run information, for the verdict step only. It carries the observed result of the run; the facts of how the run was arranged, enough to judge whether it was arranged to succeed under the operational test in step 5; when each lock was fixed relative to the run (before the run, after the run, or never fixed), as stated by whoever offers the run; the dated written plan record, with its fixed terms (the hypothesis outcome, the falsifying result, the metric, and the selector criteria, and the contested, load-bearing assumptions listed in the plan) and its recording time; the run time; and the name of the assumption the run tested, as stated by whoever offers the run. Where the plan was revised, input 4 carries the latest version recorded before the run, with per-lock timestamps, or the full version history with the term content of each version, not only the latest record. A single-version plan carrying one overall recording date and time suffices as that version's per-lock evidence for every lock it contains. The version in which a lock first appears alone does not suffice where a later version recorded before the run changed that lock's terms, because that version carries the superseded terms, not the latest pre-run terms. Where input 4 carries only a post-run revised record, with no pre-run version or version history showing the pre-run terms, the pre-run terms are not supplied, with the consequence step 5 states for unverifiable timing. Where input 4 names no assumption, the gate check cannot show the assumption listed as contested and load-bearing, with the consequence step 5 states for a gate failure. The stated timing is checked against the evidence rule in step 5: the dated written record of the test plan compared with the run time, and the dated record overrides stated timing where they conflict. Where no dated written record of the plan exists, or no run time is recorded, the timing of a lock cannot be verified and the fixed terms are not supplied, with the consequences step 5 states. The plan record is the source of the fixed terms at the judging entry point, and the run time is the value the timing check in step 5 compares the record against. The plan record is taken as given: the skill makes no authenticity check on it, and trusts it for the fixed terms, the gate listing, and the timing. That is a recorded limitation, not a check the agent performs.

What the skill does when an input is missing:

- Stop rule 1: if no contested, load-bearing assumption can be named, the skill does not run. It says so and stops. There is no test to design, and it does not go looking for something to test. If the context given does not let the agent tell whether an assumption is load-bearing, stop rule 1 applies: no assumption can then be named as load-bearing, so the skill does not run.
- Stop rule 2: if a hypothesis, metric, falsifying result, or selector criteria cannot be fixed before the run, the run must not proceed as an experiment. The skill says so: at best it is a demo, and a demo is not evidence the idea holds. This rule applies only when a future run is being designed, at the design entry point.

The stop rules are exits, not branches inside the procedure. Each stop rule fires at the point its missing input is discovered, before the run, and ends the work instead of choosing a different path. Stop rule 1 fires when step 1 cannot name a contested, load-bearing assumption. Stop rule 2 fires when step 2 or step 3 cannot fix a lock, including when step 2 finds a vague hypothesis or an impossible or unobservable falsifying result. An exit ends the work; it does not branch the procedure.

The skill has two entry points, and an entry point is a starting point, not a branch. The design entry point runs steps 1 to 5 in order, for a run that has not happened yet. The judging entry point starts at step 5, for a completed run offered as proof, using input 4 only. At the judging entry point, "using input 4 only" means the fixed terms, the run time, the arrangement facts, and the stated timing all arrive as part of input 4, and inputs 1 to 3 are not consulted and no other supply route is used. Steps 1 to 4 do not apply retrospectively to a run that already happened: the agent does not redesign the test or re-fix locks after the run. Stop rule 2 applies only at the design entry point, when a future run is being designed; it does not fire at the judging entry point, where the run has already happened and the locks are judged as they stand. Stop rule 1 does not fire at the judging entry point either, because step 1 never runs there and no test is being designed; the judging entry point judges the run offered, it does not name assumptions to test. The contested and load-bearing gate and the step 2 and step 3 quality bars are still applied at the judging entry point, but as checks inside step 5, not as step 1 or as stop rules: step 5 checks the gate from the plan record supplied in input 4, and checks the quality bars against the fixed terms as recorded in the latest version of that plan recorded before the run, and a failure of either check yields no verdict under the precedence order in step 5, never a pass.

## Output form

The output is a fixed written form, in two parts, in this order:

1. Test plan, recorded before the run: the contested, load-bearing assumptions listed; for each, a falsifiable test with hypothesis, metric, and falsifying result; and the selector criteria fixed in advance.
2. Verdict, after the run, in step 5's precedence order: first "demo, not an experiment", second no verdict, third pass or fail, on the grounds and under the conditions step 5 states. The verdict rests on the terms in the latest version of the plan recorded before the run; a post-run change to a lock's terms is disregarded, as step 5 states. Where there is no verdict, the record names the first ground that applies in step 5's record order and gives no pass or fail. A fresh test, with new terms fixed before its run, is the only way to test the idea again.

The skill does not drift into free prose. The plan comes first, the verdict second, and the verdict never re-describes the terms.

At the judging entry point, the output form is the verdict alone in the sense that this skill run produces no new test plan: the output is the reproduction of the prior plan as supplied in input 4, with its fixed terms, its recording time, and its listed assumptions, followed by the verdict in the precedence order above, using the same verdict wording and the same no-verdict records. Where no prior plan was supplied in input 4, the output is the verdict plus a statement that no plan was supplied, so the demo judgement on the unverifiable timing ground is the verdict.

## Worked example

Input:

- Claim: "Adding a guided onboarding checklist to the signup flow will cut new-account support tickets by 20 percent in the first month."
- Contested, load-bearing assumption: "new users do not find the help articles". Priya states disagreement with this assumption, with the stated reason that support logs show users open the articles but abandon them at step 3. The checklist idea depends on users failing to find help: the checklist is the route to help for users who cannot find the help articles, so if users already find them the checklist cannot cut tickets.
- Context: tickets are counted from the support queue for accounts less than 30 days old.
- Plan recorded: 2026-11-10 09:00 CST. Run time: 2026-12-17 09:00 CST.
- Observed result: support tickets from checklist accounts fell 22 percent against control accounts in the first 30 days, observed after the enrolment window closed: the last enrolled account, created 2026-11-16, completed its 30 days on 2026-12-16.
- Arrangement facts: accounts were assigned by the pre-fixed selector rule before results were known, and the setup allowed failure.

Output:

Test plan, recorded 2026-11-10 09:00 CST:

- Contested, load-bearing assumption: new users do not find the help articles.
- Hypothesis: support tickets from checklist accounts fall at least 20 percent against control accounts in the first 30 days.
- Metric: count of support tickets opened per account in the first 30 days, counted from the support queue, split by checklist versus control group.
- Falsifying result: tickets from checklist accounts are equal to or higher than tickets from control accounts in the same 30-day window.
- Selector criteria: include every new account created from 2026-11-10 09:00 CST to 2026-11-16, assigned alternately by creation order to checklist or control; exclude accounts created by staff and accounts that cancel within 24 hours for a billing error.

Each lock was fixed before the run, timestamped 2026-11-10 09:00 CST.

Verdict: pass, recorded 2026-12-17 09:00 CST. The hypothesis outcome was observed: tickets from checklist accounts fell 22 percent against control accounts, which is at least the 20 percent fall fixed in the hypothesis. No demo condition applies, the gate passes, and the quality bars pass. The verdict is judged only against the terms fixed before the run.

## Second worked example

Judging entry point, input 4 only:

- Claim: "The new weekly digest email lifts repeat visits."
- Assumption the run tested, as stated by whoever offers the run: existing readers do not notice new posts.
- Plan record: version 1 recorded 2026-09-07 09:00 CST, listing that assumption as contested and load-bearing, with hypothesis, metric, and falsifying result fixed and no selector criteria; version 2 recorded 2026-09-21 09:00 CST, adding selector criteria.
- Run time: 2026-09-14 09:00 CST to 2026-09-20 09:00 CST.
- Observed result: repeat visits from digest readers rose 18 percent against non-readers in the run week, which matches the hypothesis outcome fixed in version 1.
- Arrangement facts: the digest was sent to subscribers chosen from those who had opened the announcement email about the digest, and the selector criteria in version 2 were written after the run, from the list of subscribers actually sent the digest.

Output:

Test plan, reproduced from the plan record in input 4:

- Contested, load-bearing assumption: existing readers do not notice new posts.
- Hypothesis: repeat visits from digest readers rise at least 15 percent against non-readers in the run week.
- Metric: count of repeat visits per reader in the run week, counted from site analytics, split by digest readers versus non-readers.
- Falsifying result: repeat visits from digest readers are equal to or lower than repeat visits from non-readers in the same week.
- Selector criteria: none in version 1; added in version 2, recorded 2026-09-21 09:00 CST: include every subscriber who opened at least one email in the prior month.

Verdict: demo, not an experiment. The selector criteria first appear in a revision recorded after the run, so that lock was fixed after the run; and the arrangement facts show the cases were subscribers who had already responded to the announcement, not cases picked by a rule fixed in advance. Either ground draws the demo judgement under step 5, and the demo judgement overrides the observed match with the hypothesis outcome, so the run is not recorded as a pass and is not evidence the idea holds.
